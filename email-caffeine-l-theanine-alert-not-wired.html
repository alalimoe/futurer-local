Alert, Not Wired

Screen fatigue is not the same as being tired.

Your attention is frayed: you are awake, but every ping pulls you off the thread you meant to hold.

More caffeine rarely fixes that.

A common move is adding L-Theanine to the coffee you already drink, so alertness feels steadier instead of scattered.

Why People Add L-Theanine to Coffee

Coffee is already your caffeine source.

It blocks adenosine, which is why you feel alert.

It can also leave you jittery and scattered when you are already context-switching all day.

L-Theanine is an amino acid people often add alongside coffee when they want that alertness without the wired edge.

In research and everyday use, the question is less “more stimulation” and more “steadier focus on one thread.”

What L-Theanine does

An amino acid associated with calmer, more focused alertness.

It is often discussed for smoothing the sharper edge that caffeine can bring, rather than adding another layer of stimulation.

Why pair with coffee

Most people already have a coffee ritual.

Adding 200mg L-Theanine with your first cup is a simple way to smooth caffeine’s sharper edge without another stimulant source.

In some focus studies, the combination was associated with steadier attention than caffeine alone for sustained screen work.

When it helps

Long screen blocks, meetings, and writing sprints when you are already drinking coffee.

Not for evenings or sleep.

Not a replacement for breaks.

If sleep matters tonight, stop coffee and other stimulants by early afternoon.

How to Use on Screen-Heavy Days

1
 Morning, with your first coffee Take 200mg L-Theanine with breakfast or your first cup.

Skip extra caffeine capsules until you know how the pair feels.
2
 Cap it early Coffee and other stimulants after about 2 p.m. often steal from sleep.

Screen fatigue tomorrow starts with how you wind down tonight.
3
 Pair with breaks Run a 90-minute focus block, then give your eyes time off the screen.

L-Theanine with coffee can help attention hold; it does not replace recovery.
