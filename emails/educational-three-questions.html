01

Immediate vs routine

When do you need the shift?

Immediate means you want a noticeable change today. A long work block. A late night that stole this morning.

Routine means you want a baseline that builds over weeks. Memory, attention, a calmer floor. You take it on ordinary days, not only hard ones.

Do not mix these in week one. An acute compound will hide what a daily one is doing.
02

Stimulating vs neutral

How should the day feel?

Stimulating adds drive and wakefulness. Use it when the problem is momentum, not what to do.

Neutral keeps the volume of the day the same, just cleaner. Calm focus. No extra push.

If you already feel wired, pick neutral. A second source of drive on top of coffee is how people decide the category “does not work.”
03

Standalone vs paired

One bottle, or a pair later?

Standalone means you can learn it alone. Take it for 7 to 14 days. Change nothing else.

Paired means the compound is designed to sit with a partner. You still buy one first. The partner comes after you know how the first one sits.

A pair on day one is two variables. You will not know which one did what.
